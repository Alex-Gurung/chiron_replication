Can a character sheet put the names back?

CHIRON's masked-character test on the NCP novels. A model sees a passage with the three principals' names replaced by ids, plus one representation of each character built only from earlier chapters, and says which id is which. 1,087 sections (about 320 words), 1,740 short spans (about 50 words) and 284 dense windows (about 900 words); five model setups (Qwen3-4B, Qwen3.5-9B base, Qwen3.8-27B with thinking off and on, Mistral-7B).

What the model sees

… ‘Magic,’ [CHAR 1] says, at the same time as [CHAR 2] says, ‘Witchcraft.’ He snorts. ‘I can see you’ve been raised in the Faith like any good Orlican. Yes, the Church certainly likes to preach on the evils of magic and spirits and summonings. But before you called us witches, we had another name: czarownik. Our gifts were revered, not reviled.’ A caving roof, [CHAR 0]’s scream, a thousand mistakes. …
dark · answer: Marysieńka = [CHAR 0], The Leszy (Eliasz Kowal) = [CHAR 1], Liska Radost = [CHAR 2]
One block per character (the representation being tested), then the passage. Without thinking, the model answers "Which ID in the passage is <name>?" and is scored from the probability of each id digit. With thinking, it reasons and then gives the whole name-to-id mapping.

Findings

  • New (Oct 1): at about 5k prompt tokens the best option is not a better sheet but a sheet plus the last 1,000 words of the story, shown once: charmem 76.1% (alone 71.9%), gpt-oss character summaries 76.7%, the Llama summary 76.8% at 4.0k tokens, on Qwen3.8-27B without thinking; the Llama notes cut to 3k words reach 77.3% with three times the tokens. Redone with gpt-oss the archive's way (its extraction prompt, filler removed, gpt-oss entailment filter), the Llama notes reach 77.4% against 80.3%. See New gpt-oss sheets.
  • Scoring changes the thinking-off results. Asked about one character at a time, Qwen3.8-27B without thinking almost never answers 0: with names only it gets 0.1% of the characters whose id is [CHAR 0], which caps it near two thirds whatever it is told (the verbatim-quote oracle scores 60.7% that way). Taking the best one-to-one mapping of its three answers (joint scoring; the prompt says each id is used once) removes this, and the same quote oracle scores 91.8%. Every thinking-off number below uses joint scoring; the per-character numbers are in Scoring.
  • Model strength decides how much the representations help. Over names only, Qwen3-4B gains 3 to 11 points, and Qwen3.8-27B without thinking 20 to 32, positive in every book. With thinking it reaches 94 to 97% on sections with any representation.
  • The models do use the sheets. With another principal's sheet and the names inside it exchanged, Qwen3.8-27B falls to 27.9% (names only 48.2%) and to 13.9% with thinking; the reasoning traces cite specific sheet facts and match them to events in the passage.
  • Exactly the information needed is enough. On 63 passages, Claude agents wrote 1 to 4 facts per character that were established before the chapter and identify it in the passage (median 45 words). They give Qwen3.8-27B 80.4% without thinking and 99.5% with it, against 72.5% and 95.2% for the whole v2 sheet on the same passages, and 88.0% and 96.6% for the full Llama notes. Clues taken from the passage itself give 92.6% and 100.0%. The gpt-oss prior-facts oracle did worse (61.1% and 88.9%): it picked generic traits.
  • The representations get different passages right. Which passage it is explains 57% of the variance in correctness and the representation 1.2%; choosing the best representation per passage would reach 95% against 81% for the best single one. Combining the Llama notes with v2 gives the best score, 81.1%.
  • The full Llama CHIRON notes win because of what they say, not their length or recency: without the previous chapter they score 79.9% (full 80.3%), and cut to 6,000 words 77.4%, still well above the gpt-oss CHIRON claims at any length (71.5% in full). The Llama notes answer CHIRON's questions once per chapter, seeing the whole chapter; the gpt-oss claims answer them per 300-word snippet as in the paper, as single claims, and keep little of what relates a character to the other principals (the Llama notes name them 4 to 7 times as often).
  • Was the gpt-oss CHIRON run done wrong? Redoing the Llama notes with gpt-oss, with the same questions once per chapter and the same layout, gives 75.4% on Qwen3.8-27B without thinking (62.3% on Qwen3.5-9B base): above the gpt-oss claims (71.5%), below the Llama notes (80.3%). So about half the gap comes from answering per 300-word snippet, which also loses first-person narrators: gpt-oss is rarely told who "I" is and marks narrators absent from most of their own snippets. The other half sits between the two sets of chapter notes; gpt-oss's are 4 to 5 times shorter than Llama's.
  • With thinking, much of every score is gender. From names alone, Qwen3.8-27B places 89% of characters whose gender is unique among the three principals and 35% when all three share one (chance 33%); the representations mostly compete on same-gender characters, where the spread widens from 86% (v2) to 94% (full Llama notes). Stating the gender adds little because the names already carry it: gender only gives 65.7% with thinking. Without thinking it does nothing: Qwen3.8-27B 47.7% (names only 48.2%), Qwen3.5-9B base 47.1% (50.7%).
  • Short spans (about 50 words) keep thinking-on accuracy below ceiling: names only 51.3%, every representation 78 to 82%, name-swapped v2 19.3%. Only the full Llama notes are clearly ahead of v2 (+3.7 points, better in 16/19 books), matching its lead on sections without thinking; on dense windows every representation is at 97 to 99%.
  • Better notes shorten the reasoning. With thinking on, the median reasoning on sections is 76k characters with names only, 18k with v2 and 8k with the full Llama notes; on dense windows, where every representation is at ceiling, v2 still needs 13k against 8k for the Llama notes.
  • Which part of a sheet matters depends on the model. For Qwen3.5-9B base the ~120-word relationships section alone (57.3%) does about as well as the whole v2 sheet (58.9%); for Qwen3.8-27B each section alone adds 7 to 12 points, and the sheet without relationships (66.6%) is close to the full sheet (68.8%).
  • Character notes make the real next passage more likely in every book: 8 to 15% lower perplexity for Qwen3.5-9B base without story context, and 1 to 4% on top of the 4,000 words before the passage (Qwen3.5-9B base and Qwen3.8-27B).

New gpt-oss sheets (Oct 1)

Goal: a gpt-oss-120b representation at about 5k prompt tokens that beats every non-oracle one on Qwen3.8-27B (thinking off). The generating model was given a writer's brief, never the evaluation.

Sheets alone did not get there. Over 30 one-call designs (layouts, note sources, lengths, a review pass, joint sheets for the three principals, a consensus of three samples) land between 64.6 and 71.7% against charmem's 71.9% at 4.4k tokens. Re-running charmem's own synthesis code on its own ledgers, with prompts byte-identical to the published run, gives 70.7% (three samples, nested: 69.3, 68.5, 69.0%). At about 1,000 words a character every reasonable design ends near 70 to 72%. One formatting detail matters: nesting a sheet's section headings under the character's name costs up to 2.5 points (charmem nested: 69.4%); rows marked “as written” keep the model's own headings.

Why the one-call sheets lose: facts land in the wrong character's sheet (who said or did what in a shared scene, kinship, who calls whom “ma'am”), and the non-thinking model follows the sheet that echoes the passage. Content shared by all three characters hurts: the latest chapter's notes (charmem + last chapter 69.1%), a list of names in the character's story (charmem + names 69.0%, names alone 39.4%, below names only, 48.2%), status blocks and event timelines.

What does beat every sheet: the last words of the story, shown once. The last 1,000 words before the passage's chapter add little alone (65.2%) but lift every sheet by 3 to 5 points: charmem 76.1% at 5.7k tokens (500 words: 75.4% at 5.0k), gpt-oss character summaries 76.7% at 5.3k, the Llama summary 76.8% at 4.0k (1,500 words: 77.1% at 4.7k), and the same kind of summary written by gpt-oss from the filtered gpt-oss notes and held to about 500 words 75.5% at 4.2k (76.0% with 1,500 words; alone 69.9%). That matches the Llama notes cut to 3k words (77.3% at 15.0k) at a third of the tokens. A gpt-oss recap of the last 2,000 words in place of the raw text gives only 72.4%, and the same text placed after the sheets instead of before them 73.0%. On Qwen3.5-9B base charmem goes from 61.2 to 66.7% (full Llama notes 67.8%), on Qwen3-4B from 45.2 to 49.1% (full Llama notes 49.1%), and with thinking from 94.5 to 95.9% with 500 words (full Llama notes 96.7%). charmem plus the last 500 to 1,000 words is the choice that holds on every model; with the chapter so far as well, Qwen3.8-27B reaches 81.7% at 7.8k tokens.

Plot summaries and NCP's Story Information. The dataset's own chapter synopses (SuperSummary, every earlier chapter) score 71.2% alone at 5.6k tokens, above any gpt-oss plot summary (68.2% at 2.5k). With character sheets: charmem + synopses 77.6% at 9.4k, character summaries + synopses 76.0%, v2 + synopses 76.7%; charmem + a 1,000-word gpt-oss plot summary 74.4% at 6.2k. NCP's whole Story Information (v2 sheets + synopses + the last two chapters) gives 79.5% at 16.7k tokens; without the sheets 74.8%, without the synopses (v2 + last two chapters) 77.6%, without the last two chapters 76.7%, the sheets alone 68.8%. NCP also gives the writer a one-line synopsis of the chapter being written; it describes the passage's own chapter, so it is an oracle here: alone 51.6%, with everything 80.5%.

Story Information from other parts. With the synopses and the last two chapters in place, the kind of sheet matters little: v2 79.5%, charmem 79.8%, gpt-oss character summaries 79.1%, the gpt-oss Llama-style summary 79.3%, the Llama summary 80.3% (as good as the full Llama notes at a quarter of the tokens); no sheet 74.8%. The plot summary matters more: with v2 and the last two chapters, the dataset's synopses 79.5%, a gpt-oss plot summary of ~1,000 words 78.2%, of ~4,000 words 76.4%, none 77.6%. Only the last 1,000 words instead of the last two chapters costs about a point and saves about 6k tokens (charmem 78.9% at 10.7k, Llama summary 79.1% at 9.1k). Fully generated (gpt-oss sheet + gpt-oss plot summary + last 1,000 words): charmem 76.4% at 7.6k, the gpt-oss Llama-style summary 75.6% at 6.1k: the gpt-oss plot summary adds about nothing on top of the recent text, where the dataset's synopses add 2 to 3 points.

The Llama notes, redone with gpt-oss, now nearly match. The archive's own extraction prompt, run with gpt-oss (one call per chapter, character and question): 75.9% at 70.4k; without “the section never mentions X” filler 77.1%; kept only where gpt-oss rates the sentence fully supported by the chapter (the archive's entailment rule) 77.4% at 40.7k, against 80.3% for Llama at 59.1k. Before, gpt-oss chapter notes reached 75.4%.

Scoring: a prior over id labels

Without thinking, each character is asked for separately and scored from the probabilities of the id digits. Per character, the answer is the most likely digit. Qwen3.8-27B without thinking almost never answers 0, so per-character scoring cannot get [CHAR 0] right; joint scoring removes most of that. Setup

Passages
Every candidate principal is named in at least two earlier chapters. Names come from hand-checked alias tables (nicknames, earlier names, secret identities); passages with an ambiguous surname or any leftover name are dropped.
Representations
All built from chapters before the passage's chapter. See "The representations" above.
Scoring
Without thinking: next-token probabilities of the id digits at temperature 0, one question per character (Qwen3-4B over all 6 block orders, the others over 3 rotations; Mistral's reply is pre-started with "[CHAR "), combined by joint scoring (the best one-to-one mapping per passage and block order). With thinking: one generation per passage and rotation (temperature 0.6, up to 32k tokens) ending in a JSON mapping; an unreadable answer counts as wrong.
Memorization
Temperature-0 continuations of the four test books reproduced no 13-word sequence (longest verbatim run 5 words).
Caveats
21 books carry the statistics and ten of them hold most passages. The passage sets cover different books (short spans 19, dense windows 13 with at least 5 passages). With thinking on, sections are near ceiling, which compresses differences between representations. 46 of the 4,113 thinking-on generations for name-swapped v2 on short spans are missing (a vLLM engine hung under memory pressure). Prompts longer than the served context (131k tokens for Qwen3.8-27B) are skipped, which drops a few combination prompts late in long books.
